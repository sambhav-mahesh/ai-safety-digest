MATS Dec 7, 2026

Uncovering Hidden Propensities in Language Models via Limited-Parameter Finetuning

Uncovering Hidden Propensities in Language Models via Limited-Parameter Finetuning Large language models can have hidden propensities: behaviours that arise only in rare contexts. These hidden propensities pose a challenge to alignment audits, which seek to uncover problematic model behaviour but are limited in scope to scenarios anticipated by the auditor. To address this challenge, we explore limited-parameter finetuning (LPF) on examples of a target behaviour. While training 3–15% of model parameters, LPF reveals whether the model has a hidden propensity, even when standard behavioural evaluations cannot. LPF succeeds on a suite of model organisms with hidden propensities (8B-14B parameters), and also surfaces naturally occurring hidden propensities in open-weight models (27-32B parameters). LPF can thus distinguish between behaviourally equivalent models, even without knowing the context in which the propensity surfaces. LPF provides a novel affordance that may be useful for auditing frontier AI models. More

“A basic case for accelerating conceptual reasoning capabilities” by Emery Cooper, Caspar Oesterheld, Chi Nguyen

Redwood Research Blog

Introduction In this post, we make the case that alignment and other important work require especially strong conceptual reasoning capabilities. By this, we roughly mean tasks that are similar to philosophy in that they aren’t straightforwardly verifiable, and progress relies on informal a priori reasoning and argumentation. We argue that differentially accelerating AI's conceptual reasoning capabilities is important. We also think it's neglected, and that the AI safety community currently underinvests in this area. We have ourselves pivoted from doing conceptual research directly to working mainly on differentially accelerating AI's conceptual reasoning. We have a shovel-ready agenda which can absorb more people. In a follow-up post, we will lay out at a high level how to work on this problem. More

Aligning MLLMs with the Latent Structure of Human Cognition via Behavior-Derived Semantic Dimensions

Ning E, Changde Du, Yizhuo Lu, Huiguang He

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have achieved impressive performance across a wide range of reasoning tasks, yet it remains unclear whether their internal representations and decision criteria align with the latent structure of human cognition. In this work, we propose a novel framework to align MLLMs with the multidimensional mental representations underlying human similarity judgements. We build on a previously established behavior-derived semantic embedding space, in which objects are represented along 66 interpretable dimensions derived from 4.70 million human Odd-One-Out (O1O) judgements. For each triplet, we infer the most salient decision dimension and convert it into a dimension-grounded linguistic rationale. This enables MLLMs to learn both the final choice and a justification consistent with the inferred behavioral criterion. Experimental results demonstrate that our approach improves model consistency with human similarity judgements while retaining competitive performance on standard multimodal benchmarks. More

AI evaluators favor job applications written by certain AI models, independent of candidate quality

Nicolò Pagan, Michael Andreas Ebling, Stefania Ionescu, Anikó Hannák

Job applicants increasingly use large language models (LLMs) to write cover letters, while employers deploy LLMs to screen them. In this all-AI setting, we ask whether the \emph{choice of model} affects hiring outcomes independently of candidate qualifications. We introduce a multi-model auditing framework pairing eleven LLM writer agents with nine API-hosted evaluator agents across ten job descriptions and fifty candidates per job. Four findings emerge. First, we identify a generative preference hierarchy: a consistent global ranking of writer models by evaluator acceptance, in which locally hosted models are systematically disadvantaged regardless of their license, compounded by self-preference bias where evaluators favour their own outputs. Second, evaluator models differ substantially in how much bias they introduce based on applicants' tool choice, offering actionable guidance for fairer screening design. More

Task Vectors Can Be Linearly Decomposed into Interpretable and Controllable Components

Yukiya Horiba, Hakaze Cho, Takahiro Yakoh

Task Vectors (TVs) provide a compact way to steer large language models, but they are typically treated as atomized directions merely extracted from hidden states, leaving their internal functional structure poorly understood. Motivated by prior work that decomposes in-context learning into Task Recognition (TR) and Task Learning (TL), we ask whether a single TV can likewise be decomposed into functionally meaningful components. We formulate a TR/TL-based decomposition from candidate-label unembeddings, separating each TV into TR, TL, and residual components, and try to optimize the TR and TL coefficients using Bayesian optimization for more effective intervention. We evaluate this formulation across multiple model families, classification tasks, and both instruction- and demonstration-based TVs. Across these settings, TR/TL-based optimization identifies intervention directions that outperform the original TVs. Although optimization over random directions can also improve performance, TR/TL-based search yields more reliable gains, particularly for instruction-based TVs. More

Mechanistic Interpretability for End-to-End Self-Driving

Manav Gagvani, Benjamin Namikas, Sivamurugan Velmurugan, Nysa Kumar, Shrey Sharma, Thomas Peterson, Pratyush Mathur, Hyunseo Chang, Rohan Paleja

End-to-end (E2E) autonomous driving models achieve strong planning performance but remain largely opaque, limiting observability and trust. We apply mechanistic interpretability to E2E driving by training Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) on the internal activations of a simple, single-camera end-to-end path planning model. Unsupervised clustering of SAE latents reveals interpretable structure aligned with route intent, vehicle speed, and braking behavior, recovering route intent with 98.3% accuracy without supervision. Through paired counterfactual image edits, we further show that specific SAE features are visually grounded and systematically respond to scene changes such as traffic light state and stop sign presence. Steering individual SAE latents produces predictable, monotonic changes in planner outputs including lateral displacement, curvature, and braking. More

QUAL: Quantile Alignment Improves Zero-Shot Time-Series Foundation Models

Junhyeok Kang, Sangjun Han, Jun Seo, Seunghan Lee, Jaehoon Lee, Dongwan Kang, Minjae Kim, Sungdong Yoo, Tae Yoon Lim, Hwanil Choi, Soonyoung Lee, Wonbin Ahn

Time-series foundation models (TSFMs) provide zero-shot forecasts for unseen series, but their accuracy varies across datasets. Combining several models can reduce this variability, yet zero-shot deployment provides no target labels for selecting or weighting them. We find that a model pool is useful not only as a final ensemble, but also as a probability reference for transforming each member forecast. Aligning quantiles through this pool-relative reference improves every member forecast in aggregate. Motivated by this finding, we propose QUAL (Quantile alignment), a simple label-free framework for improving and combining diverse TSFMs. QUAL first produces aligned member forecasts, then applies Huber aggregation and rearrangement to obtain a robust combined forecast without training or model modification. On GIFT-Eval, quantile alignment reduces average member MASE and CRPS by 7.4% and 9.8%. The full framework remains robust when weak forecasters enter a candidate pool whose quality cannot be verified in advance. More

ShallowDefend: Harnessing Shallow Safety Alignment for Multi-Turn Defender

Qiyao Ma, Xiaodong Liu, Weiwei Yang, Mingqian Feng, Chengquan Guo, Jialin Song, Carolyn Buractaon, Zhe Zhao, Junshan Zhang, Sandeep Atluri, Christopher White, Jianfeng Gao

Safety alignment has enabled large language models to reliably refuse harmful requests directly, yet the same request distributed across a multi-turn conversation still succeeds far too often. This brittleness has a mechanistic root: Alignment is shallow, with safety behavior concentrated in a model's first few output tokens, and existing defenses inherit the weakness, supervising the surface form of the response without ever giving the model an explicit safety decision to learn. We take the opposite view of shallowness: if a short span determines the trajectory of an entire response, that span is the highest-leverage place to intervene, and the defender can occupy it first. We propose Assess $\to$ Decide $\to$ Respond, a framework in which the defender assesses the user's intent, commits to an explicit, parseable safety decision, and only then generates a reply conditioned on it, with the commitment trained by reinforcement learning at the payload turn. Efficient Scalable Oversight via Rewinding

Ziyi Guan, Yael Tauman Kalai, Ron Rothblum, Zoe Xi

Scalable oversight asks how a weak verifier can check the outputs of computations performed by more powerful AI systems. One proposed approach uses relativizing interactive proofs, in which a weak verifier checks the output of a complex oracle-aided computation by interacting with one or more powerful provers. A prominent example is debate, which is highly efficient but assumes that one of two powerful AI models, or provers, is truthful. For certain classes of oracle-aided computations, this assumption can be removed, but at substantially greater computational cost. In this work, we introduce \emph{rewindable interactive proofs} (RIPs), a new model of relativizing interactive proofs for scalable oversight. In an RIP, the verifier may rewind the prover to an earlier state of the interaction and continue from there. More

How I Aligned Your Model: Scaling Up Multilingual Safety Training with Templates

Max Neuwinger, Hanna Yukhymenko, Imanol Schlag

Safety alignment for Large Language Models is heavily focused on English with limited multilingual representation, particularly damaging low-resource language performance. Related open training datasets are often limited in size or do not follow region-localized harmful topics to broaden the scope of alignment beyond cultures. We propose a scalable template-based augmentation pipeline to produce massive multilingual refusal and benign corpora, extracting safe templates from harmful prompts, translating them and then filling in the slots with culturally authentic entities, both harmful and benign. As a result, we release MSafeTemp, a validated corpus spanning 60 languages useful for multilingual safety alignment and evaluation. Using the dataset, we show that English-only evaluation misjudges multilingual safety: for several models, attacks that fail in English succeed once translated, while culturally localized variants expose further failures that translation alone misses. A Low-Rank Space Captures Linear Alignment Between LLM and Human Neural Activity During Naturalistic Language Listening

Zach Paris, Emily Finn

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used as computational models of human language processing, yet it remains unclear which components of their representations drive alignment with neural activity. Here, we use ridge reduced-rank regression to identify the brain-predictive subspace of LLM representations: the representational directions that support linear prediction of neural responses. Across three naturalistic language-listening datasets spanning intracranial electrophysiology and fMRI, and six model conditions spanning autoregressive, bidirectional masked, and randomly initialized language models, we find that full linear encoding performance can be recovered using only 1-3% of the original embedding dimensionality. These low-dimensional subspaces generalize across held-out participants and, to a lesser extent, across datasets, suggesting that neural predictivity is concentrated in a compact and partially shared representational space. We next compare these brain-predictive directions with the principal axes of variation in the embeddings. More

Voice-cloning TTS models synthesize an unseen speaker's voice from brief speech prompts, but it remains unclear which acoustic properties they represent and use. We introduce a feature-based interpretability framework for autoregressive voice-cloning TTS models, analyzing features from sparse autoencoders (SAEs), PCA, and random directions via sparse probing, steering, and quantitative auto-interpretability. We focus on Qwen3-TTS and extend probing and steering to $5$ additional models to test generalization across architectures. These models encode concepts like pitch, energy, noise, and accent, but sparse prediction and steering are effective only for some concepts. Strong probing does not always translate to steering or vice-versa. SAEs are useful for sparse probing of human-labelled concepts and novel-concept discovery, while PCA is competitive for dense probing and steering. Auto-interpretability finds $684$ automatically validated SAE features in Qwen3-TTS covering speaker clusters, speaking style, and recording conditions beyond known concepts. More

A Negotiation-Aware Hybrid LLM Framework for Intent Interpretation and Service Discovery

Wided Meflah, Hayet Brabra, Walid Gaaloul

Increasingly, users express their needs as natural-language intents rather than structured requests. These intents may be incomplete, high-level, or internally conflicting. A conflicting intent either violates fundamental properties of the cloud paradigm or states mutually inconsistent requirements. Classical composition approaches are designed for structured, low-level, and complete requests. They cannot interpret such intents, discover suitable services from provider catalogs, or negotiate conflicting requirements with users. To address this gap, we propose a negotiation-aware framework for intent interpretation and service discovery. The framework first refines the natural-language intent and dynamically detects conflicts. It then structures the intent into a provider-independent TOSCA template. For each specification, it retrieves candidate service offers through deterministic matching grounded in cloud provider offerings. Some conflicts arise during interpretation. Other requirements prove unsatisfiable during discovery. Both are resolved through iterative natural-language negotiation with the user. More

Revisiting Over-refusal in Safety-aligned Large Language Models: A Representation and Optimization Perspective

Chao Chen, Pengyang Shao, Wei Qin, Shijie Hao, Fei Shen, Richang Hong

Over-refusal is a critical side effect of safety alignment in large language models (LLMs), causing harmless but superficially risky queries to be mistakenly rejected. Despite the effectiveness of existing mitigation methods, why safety-aligned LLMs may still reject harmless queries under correct safety supervision remains insufficiently understood. To address this gap, we analyze over-refusal through the lens of representation and optimization. We make two observations: 1) the representations of harmful and over-refusal samples are visually distinguishable at certain intermediate layers but increasingly overlap in deeper layers; and 2) harmful-sample gradients exhibit high intra-class similarity and concentrate in a low-rank subspace. Moreover, they are more strongly aligned with refusal-target gradients than with correct helpful-target gradients on over-refusal prompts. More

Improving the Intrinsic Interpretability of Differentiable Decision Trees through Euclidean Feature-Concentration Regularisation

Qiqi Yang

Differentiable decision trees (DDTs) support end-to-end optimisation and explicit decision paths, but dense oblique splits can obscure the role of individual input features. We propose the Euclidean-Concentrated Differentiable Decision Tree (EU-DDT), which regularises the relative absolute feature weights at each internal node using their normalised squared Euclidean distance from the uniform distribution. This encourages dominance by a small number of features while retaining probabilistic soft routing. Across five matched random seeds, EU-DDT reduces the mean effective number of features from 8.94 to 1.54 on Wine and from 45.28 to 9.06 on Digits. Mean test accuracy changes from 100.0% to 93.9% on Wine and from 92.3% to 94.6% on Digits, illustrating the dataset-dependent trade-off between concentration and predictive performance. We also use EU-DDT to parameterise a PPO actor with an MLP critic. Two random-seed runs per environment show a consistent qualitative increase in concentration while preserving effective task performance. More

Interpretable by Design Tabular Prediction via In-Context Local Linear Learning

Ran Eisenberg, Steven Berger, Ofir Lindenbaum

In-context learning (ICL) lets tabular foundation models make predictions without dataset-specific training or fine-tuning. These models achieve strong predictive accuracy, but interpreting how individual features contribute to the prediction remains difficult. In biology, for example, feature contributions can help identify biomarkers or risk genes. Post-hoc interpretability methods assign feature importance scores, but these scores do not indicate what the model actually uses and can lead to explanation errors. We introduce TICL$^3$ (Tabular In-Context Local Linear Learning), an interpretable-by-design predictor that uses a tabular foundation model to guide a simple linear prediction rule. TICL$^3$ first learns a shared linear model to capture dataset-level feature effects. Then, for each query, it retrieves similar training samples using representations from the foundation model and fits a second linear model to the residuals in that neighborhood. The shared model captures broad effects, while the local model corrects errors among similar samples. More

Interpretable Feature Discovery from Unstructured Data Using LLMs and Statistical Choice Models

Baptiste Arnaudo, Amine Bennouna, Sébastien Martin

Feature extraction from unstructured data typically relies on opaque embeddings unsuited to interpretable decision-making. Interpretable features, on the other hand, typically require costly, and often infeasible, hand-engineering. Large language models (LLMs) offer a natural bridge, as they can read raw data and describe it in interpretable terms. One could simply ask an LLM to extract features from a large sample of the dataset, but context limitations and diluted attention can lead to unsatisfactory results. We introduce a method that automatically discovers interpretable, decision-relevant features from unstructured data. Interpretable neural equation learning for high-dimensional materials data

Equation learning seeks to discover compact, interpretable equations from data using various machine-learning techniques. However, few workflows discover and interpret learned equations in sparse, high-dimensional materials datasets. We present nn-EQL, an equation-learning framework that generates candidate equations through iterative training, pruning, and retraining of a neural network with customizable, user-specified neuron-level activation functions. Candidate equations are ranked using a minimum description length (MDL)-inspired score, balancing error and sparsity, and diagnosed to assess term-wise contributions, input-scaled sensitivities, and uncertainties. We demonstrate nn-EQL on standard AI-Feynman benchmarks as well as multiscale high-dimensional materials datasets such as finite-element simulations of heterogeneous materials, and high-throughput experimental thin-film deposition data. nn-EQL integrates with tabular data and high-dimensional data via standard encoder models, learning equations and providing insight into learned terms. Looking forward, integrating physically interpretable representations, multimodal and multi-fidelity data, and adaptive data acquisition could enable equation learning to formulate and test mechanistic hypotheses. However, few workflows discover and interpret learned equations in sparse, high-dimensional materials datasets. We present nn-EQL, an equation-learning framework that generates candidate equations through iterative training, pruning, and retraining of a neural network with customizable, user-specified neuron-level activation functions. Candidate equations are ranked using a minimum description length (MDL)-inspired score, balancing error and sparsity, and diagnosed to assess term-wise contributions, input-scaled sensitivities, and uncertainties. We demonstrate nn-EQL on standard AI-Feynman benchmarks as well as multiscale high-dimensional materials datasets such as finite-element simulations of heterogeneous materials, and high-throughput experimental thin-film deposition data. nn-EQL integrates with tabular data and high-dimensional data via standard encoder models, learning equations and providing insight into learned terms. Looking forward, integrating physically interpretable representations, multimodal and multi-fidelity data, and adaptive data acquisition could enable equation learning to formulate and test mechanistic hypotheses.

Human-Residual Steering: Aligning Frozen LLM Judges with Human Judgment Distributions

Chen Lyu, Phong Do, Gabriele Pergola

Large language models (LLMs) are widely used as automated judges, yet most alignment methods target aggregated human judgments and overlook variation among individual annotators. Existing approaches to matching the full distribution of human ratings mainly rely on output-level calibration or parameter fine-tuning. We propose Human-Residual Steering (HRS), to our knowledge the first method to align frozen LLM judges with human judgment distributions through learned hidden-state interventions. An oracle analysis shows that low-rank corrections at a single layer remove most of the discrepancy between judge and human distributions. HRS learns to predict such corrections from the judge's hidden representation and rating logits, training on individual annotations with proper scoring rules for ordinal and nominal judgments and optimising through the frozen downstream layers. Across 14 benchmarks and four judges from three model families, HRS outperforms all evaluated frozen-weight baselines. More
