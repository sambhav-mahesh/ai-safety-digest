Redwood Research Oct 23, 2026

“Simulated Control Evaluations” by Jeffrey Olmo, Tyler

Redwood Research Blog

We’ve released a paper on simulated control evaluations (SCEs). They’re a method for testing whether the monitors AI developers use to oversee their agents would catch a model deliberately trying to cause harm, under conditions much closer to real deployment than current tests allow. Developers have started running monitors in their agentic deployments: models that flag or block an agent's actions when they look dangerous. To know whether a monitor is good enough, you want to know how often it would stop a model that was actively trying to do something harmful, such as quietly inserting a security vulnerability, exfiltrating credentials, or sabotaging an experiment. The standard way to measure this is a control evaluation: instruct a model to attempt such attacks, and measure how often the monitor catches it before an attack succeeds. More

Where Rounding Happens Matters: Rounding-Location Alignment Stabilizes LLM Reinforcement Learning

Yijing Ke, Tianle Cai, Ruosong Wang

Reinforcement learning for large language models often uses separate systems for inference and training. Even with identical weights and BF16 tensors, implementation differences can make an intended on-policy update off-policy and destabilize training. Existing remedies align the entire inference and training stacks, change global precision, or modify the learning algorithm. We ask whether aligning selected parts of the execution stack can stabilize BF16 RL while other implementation differences remain. We align training-side rounding locations with inference in three regions---RoPE, SwiGLU, and residual RMSNorm---by retaining selected intermediates in FP32 until their BF16 outputs. Across four Qwen-family models, aligning all three regions prevents the collapse observed under the default BF16 baseline; RoPE-path alignment alone also suffices in the studied runs, despite remaining numerical discrepancies. The interventions keep the inference engine, matrix-multiplication and attention kernels, and learning objective unchanged. More

AI Alignment for Digital Democracy: Fairer Voters Representation against LLM Biases

Yi Qi, Srijoni Majumdar, Evangelos Pournaras

AI representation is emerging in digital democracy, assisting in voter emulation and decision-making. Large language models (LLMs) behave differently depending on the model, the prompt context, and the voter information they receive, representing some groups more accurately than others and potentially making the emulation unfair to certain groups. We investigate demographic biases in LLM-emulated electorates: how accurately LLMs represent individual votes, whether some voter groups are represented more accurately than others, and whether AI alignment can reduce these disparities without sacrificing overall accuracy. Using three U.S. presidential elections, we compare a closed-source and an open-source LLM across voters' demographic features and test AI alignment strategies toward fairer voter representation. Removing gender from the voter profile reduces the largest group error of the models by 22-42%, and removing education reduces it by 64%, whereas removing self-reported race increases the mean group error by over 40%, all without lowering accuracy. Reading Between the Layers: Cross-Modal Alignment and Representational Hierarchy in Vision Transformers

Sugam Karki, Raúl Vázquez, Yova Kementchedjhieva

While large vision-language models excel at optical character recognition, it remains unclear how their underlying vision encoders process text and its meaning, and whether they support anything resembling reading comprehension. We systematically study transformer-based vision encoders, using cross-modal alignment between rendered text and visual concepts together with probing and retrieval-based analyses as a controlled window into these broader capabilities. Our layer-wise analyses span orthographic, grammatical, and semantic organization, and reveal that strong cross-modal mapping emerges only in the deepest layers, after unimodal object and text recognition have already developed, coinciding with a progression from surface-level to semantic representations. Although this hierarchy is strongest in contrastively trained models, larger self-supervised models and text-rich training data partially support similar behaviors without explicit language supervision. Together, our results suggest that increasing model and training-data scale, as well as text-rich training data, can partially compensate for the absence of language supervision. More

CFBrainAug: Modularity-Induced Counterfactual Brain Network Augmentation for Interpretable fMRI-based Autism Diagnosis

Yifan Qiao, Sheng Li

Machine learning on resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (rs-fMRI) brain networks is promising for diagnosing Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), but existing methods are prone to overfitting on limited training data, and their post-hoc statistical attribution offers limited clinical interpretability. We propose CFBrainAug, a counterfactual brain network learning framework that unifies data augmentation and counterfactual explanation by learning label-altered brain networks. Unlike conventional label-preserving augmentation, CFBrainAug first retrieves a label-opposite yet topologically similar candidate and then refines it under a module-number constraint. Jointly training on factual and counterfactual networks enriches the training distribution around the decision boundary, enabling a lightweight feature-selection-based SVM to perform competitively with recent graph deep learning methods. Meanwhile, the counterfactuals provide intrinsic explanations by identifying the connectivity differences between each subject and its counterfactual. More

FSVL: Taxonomic Fast-Slow Vision-to-Language Collaborative Framework for Interpretable Confusable Fish Species Identification and Knowledge Generation in the Middle Yangtze River

Meng Liang, Fucheng Wang, Lang Zhao, Jun Chen, Ziying Li, Yuxiang Zhao, Haozhuang Chi, Wenkai Fang, Qiong Zhou, Junlin Yang, Jinming Wu

The Yangtze River Basin is a critical region for freshwater biodiversity conservation. The implementation of the Yangtze River Ten-Year Fishing Ban has increased the importance of effective aquatic biodiversity monitoring and species-level identification. However, fine-grained identification of closely related freshwater fish species remains challenging due to subtle morphological differences. Existing deep learning methods often struggle to balance classification accuracy and inference efficiency, while LLMs may generate unsupported information when producing specialized biological knowledge. To address these challenges, this study proposes FSVL, a fast-slow dual-pathway framework integrating visual recognition with LLM-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). A lightweight ResNet50 fast path performs preliminary classification across 51 fish species in the Middle Yangtze River Basin and routes predefined groups of easily confused species to a slow path for refined identification. Aligning Large Language Models with Human Feedback: Mathematical Foundations and Algorithm Design

Siliang Zeng, Luca Viano, Chenliang Li, Jiaxiang Li, Volkan Cevher, Markus Wulfmeier, Stefano Ermon, Alfredo Garcia, Mingyi Hong

This article provides an introduction to the mathematical foundations and algorithmic frameworks used to align Large Language Models (LLMs) with human intentions, preferences, and values. We discuss standard alignment techniques, such as fine-tuning (SFT), reinforcement learning with human feedback (RLHF), and direct preference optimization (DPO). We also explore the theoretical underpinnings of learning from human preferences, drawing connections to inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) and discrete choice models. We present state-of-the-art algorithms in a tutorial style, discuss their advantages and limitations, and offer insights into practical implementation. Our exposition is intended to serve as a comprehensive resource for researchers and practitioners, providing both a foundational understanding of alignment methodologies and a framework for developing more robust and scalable alignment techniques. Yunfei Xie, Howard H. Qian, Shiyi Lan, Kaiyu Hang, Chen Wei

Robot foundation models are typically evaluated through costly closed-loop rollouts, motivating low-cost offline proxies for model capability. The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) suggests that as representations improve, modalities such as vision and language converge toward a shared statistical model of reality. We ask whether motion trajectories form another such modality: as embodied models become more capable, should their sensory representations converge toward the structure of physical motion? To test this, we propose Sensorimotor Alignment ($S^2$), a training-free offline metric that measures how well a model's sensory representation aligns with the geometry of physical motion. We find that $S^2$ correlates positively with downstream robot task success across model families, making it a low-cost offline proxy for prioritizing models before closed-loop evaluation. $S^2$ can also identify promising vision-language model (VLM) backbones before costly fine-tuning into vision-language-action models (VLAs). Large Language Models (LLMs) offer a promising mechanism for translating structured clinical information into readable narratives, but this process may introduce semantic drift, where clinically precise meaning is distorted during simplification. We investigate evidence-grounded health summarization for menstrual health monitoring using a constrained generation framework that combines structured evidence conditioning with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) using a prompt-based constrained generation framework. Post-Hoc Feature Selection Layer for Neural Networks Interpretability

João Kenji Suwa, João Pedro Silveira e Silva, Bruno Iochins Grisci

The interpretability of complex neural networks remains a critical challenge, especially for models already deployed in high-stakes domains. To address this, we adapted the Feature Selection Layer (FSL) to optimize feature weights post-training, a variant we term post-hoc FSL. Our approach reframes the FSL as a lightweight, trainable module that integrates with already frozen pre-trained models on tabular datasets to highlight the features the original model considers most important. This post-hoc FSL learns feature relevance by fine-tuning its weights based on the original model's learned outputs. Crucially, this process is non-invasive, operating without altering the original model's architecture or its learned parameters. We conducted our experiments using both statistical and visual metrics, including accuracy, F1 score, recall, precision, weighted t-SNE and silhouette score, and also analyzed the stability of the post-hoc FSL on high-dimensional synthetic and real-world tabular datasets. MQAScore: Fine-Grained Text-Music Alignment Evaluation via Large Audio Language Model

Wei-Jaw Lee, Chun-Ping Wang, Xuanjun Chen, Yi-Hsuan Yang

Text-to-music alignment is commonly evaluated by the global similarity between text and audio embeddings, which collapses all described attributes into a single score and obscures which ones are missing. We instead introduce MQAScore, which verifies each atomic musical concept along five clip-level dimensions (genre, instrument, mood/theme, vocal, and usage) by the probability that a large audio language model (LALM) answers affirmatively to a binary question. To assess evaluator reliability, we construct verification labels from presence/absence annotations and LLM-guided concept substitutions, and propose the Sensitivity-Invariance Ratio, which measures how selectively an evaluator responds to meaning-changing substitutions. Experiments show that concept-level scoring improves both question-answering and similarity-based evaluators, and MQAScore can be a more reliable and interpretable fine-grained complement to global similarity for evaluating text-to-music alignment. Long-text image-text alignment is essential for fine-grained scene understanding, yet existing methods mainly focus on extending context length or improving local alignment. We identify an overlooked failure mode, saliency collapse, where models over-rely on text that features visual prominence while underutilizing non-salient context. Consequently, retrieval performance degrades sharply when salient content is reduced, despite that the non-salient context remains informative. To address this, we propose RoSA, a saliency-aware finetuning framework that decomposes image-text pairs into complementary salient and contextual views, enabling joint learning of prominent and contextual semantics. We employ auxiliary objectives to explicitly align these views, ensuring robust representation with both salient and contextual features. Current user recommendation systems are often built on profile similarity or social signals such as shared connections, which are effective in friend-oriented social networks where users tend to connect with similar people or within existing social circles. What Lies Beyond Perception: A Partition Theory of the Limits of AI Evaluation and Oversight

Ahmed M. Mahran

Replacing the judge can move a benchmark score, and models can be trained to hide capabilities. Both put the evaluator in question: what can it certify? We develop a partition theory of evaluation from one principle: judgement is bounded by perception. An agent's discrimination is the partition of its inputs into classes it cannot tell apart. Discrimination is necessary for capability but not sufficient, so bounds on it bear directly on whether evaluation can show that a model lacks an ability. We prove a one-sided bound. An examinee whose partition refines its evaluator's has the discrimination to pass every test the evaluator can grade, provided each input has an acceptable reply. We define the echo test, which accepts any reply the evaluator perceives as identical to the input, and show that it is complete for the tests the evaluator can grade. More
