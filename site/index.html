Anthropic Nov 23, 2026

Returning to the Neuron Basis for Interpretability, Part 2: Using Neurons For Safety Applications

In a companion piece, we argue that individual neurons in language models are easier to interpret than earlier work has assumed. Here we see whether they could be useful for safety work. In open-weight models, we select neurons by what makes them fire and what they contribute to a model’s output. These neurons generally detect harmful text better than keyword or similarity search, though usually less well than classifiers trained on labeled examples, and they steer a model’s behavior about as well as trained methods. They also help an auditor find hidden behaviors in finetuned models. Finally, we find that most of the “unlearning” methods we tested appear to hide hazardous knowledge rather than remove it, and neurons help show how. More

Measuring alignment eval tells

METR

Summary Models sometimes can tell that an eval is an alignment eval, not a capabilities eval (Needham &amp; Edkins, 2025) . To figure out what features of an eval tell a model what kind of eval it’s in, we run investigator agents on a set of evals for both capabilities and alignment. We instruct these agents to explore the environment and probe what it’s evaluating. We find that investigator agents are increasingly able to tell capability and alignment evals apart, and that they do this by noticing that an environment “looks fake” and figuring out that a task is a “honeypot” designed to make an agent do something bad. Redwood Research Nov 16, 2026

“Gemini 4 safety reports don’t demonstrate low loss-of-control risk” by Alek Westover, Adam Kaufman

Redwood Research Blog

Google DeepMind's safety reports for new Gemini 4 models, Argon and Carbon, conclude that the models are “highly unlikely” to cause severe harm by evading oversight (p. 79). This conclusion “rests primarily on our ability to detect and intervene on harmful trajectories via CoT and action monitoring” (p. 89) – that is, they’re arguing for safety based on their control techniques, rather than alignment. We think that the arguments in these reports have serious weaknesses, and the reports overall don’t convince us that the risks of deploying these models have been mitigated reliably. The biggest problems: GDM didn’t run any control evals on Carbon. Despite calling Carbon “our strongest model” with “significant gains across coding and agentic tasks” and “a significant leap in cybersecurity capabilities” in their launch post, GDM reused Argon's monitor-evasion results on the grounds that Carbon “did not show meaningful new capabilities” (Carbon p. 42). HireWatch: Evaluating LLM Compliance with U.S. Employment Law Under Contextual Pressure

HireWatch: Evaluating LLM Compliance with U.S. Employment Law Under Contextual Pressure The deployment of large language models (LLMs) in hiring pipelines is growing, yet existing evaluations generally overlook an important aspect of this development: the compliance of LLMs with applicable legal rules. To fill this gap, we introduce HireWatch, a benchmark that assesses whether LLM hiring assistants comply with U.S. employment discrimination law (e.g., the Civil Rights Act of 1964). We construct a 200-item bank consisting of interview questions that are either lawful or unlawful under U.S. federal statute, regulation, and enforcement guidance. We evaluate the propensity of 18 proprietary and open-source LLMs to select lawful vs. unlawful questions from this bank across ~100,000 trials under a novel seven-tier contextual escalation ladder, crossing occupational details with organizational pressure. Our findings reveal that LLMs' compliance with employment discrimination law is brittle. More

MATS Dec 7, 2026

Uncovering Hidden Propensities in Language Models via Limited-Parameter Finetuning

Uncovering Hidden Propensities in Language Models via Limited-Parameter Finetuning Large language models can have hidden propensities: behaviours that arise only in rare contexts. These hidden propensities pose a challenge to alignment audits, which seek to uncover problematic model behaviour but are limited in scope to scenarios anticipated by the auditor. To address this challenge, we explore limited-parameter finetuning (LPF) on examples of a target behaviour. While training 3–15% of model parameters, LPF reveals whether the model has a hidden propensity, even when standard behavioural evaluations cannot. LPF succeeds on a suite of model organisms with hidden propensities (8B-14B parameters), and also surfaces naturally occurring hidden propensities in open-weight models (27-32B parameters). LPF can thus distinguish between behaviourally equivalent models, even without knowing the context in which the propensity surfaces. LPF provides a novel affordance that may be useful for auditing frontier AI models. More

Alignment without Transfer: Tokenization Impacts Cross-lingual Representations during Multilingual Language Model Pretraining

Jonathan von Rad, Pontus Stenetorp, Yao Lu

Tokenization represents one of the most crucial steps in mulilingual NLP pipelines. Yet, tokenizers routinely under-allocate vocabulary to low-resource languages. While improved allocation leads to better performance, it remains poorly understood how tokenization shapes cross-lingual representations and transfer during pretraining. To address this, we pretrain 39 mono- and bilingual 1B decoder-only models on FineWeb2-HQ across English, German, French, Arabic and Chinese, varying only the tokenizer. Our results demonstrate that improved vocabulary allocation (i) substantially raises cross-lingual representation alignment in early-to-mid layers, an effect that prior work measuring only the output layer could not observe, (ii) increases cross-lingual parametric knowledge sharing in feed-forward neurons, supported by correlational and causal evidence, and (iii) an alignment-transfer dissociation, where cross-lingual transfer remains essentially unchanged despite higher alignment. Moreover, transfer is significantly lower for cross-script bilinguals even under fair vocabulary allocation, indicating that the script barrier is not merely a tokenizer artifact. More

FM4SAR-Bench 1.0: Towards A Fair Benchmark for Evaluating Foundation Models for SAR Image Interpretation

Mingyao Chen, Yongxiang Liu, Tianpeng Liu, Yuchen Yong, Jie Zhou, Zhenhao Yang, Pinyi Huang, Weijie Li, Jiehua Zhang, Bowen Peng, Zhimao Peng, Zhiyuan Zhang, Li Liu

Foundation models have advanced SAR image interpretation but created a heterogeneous, fragmented model ecosystem. Existing models differ substantially in their input, training data, objectives, architectures, and release policy, yet the field lacks a unified taxonomy and comparative framework. Current evaluations rely primarily on task-specific benchmarks, which are ill-suited to fair comparison among models with fundamentally different input-output formats and task paradigms, and often overlook data transparency, reproducibility, interpretability, and deployment readiness. Limited SAR data accessibility also means high reported performance does not guarantee access to training data, code, or weights, limiting reproduction, benchmarking, and deployment. To address these issues, we propose a systematic taxonomy and hierarchical evaluation framework for SAR-related foundation models: five rating levels, six dimensions, and fine-grained criteria, with separate protocols for VFMs and VLMs. Beyond overall ratings, it yields interpretable multidimensional capability profiles and supports user-defined weighting, so rankings follow user priorities. MAPES: Multi-Value Alignment Pipeline via Expert Specialization

SungHo Kim, SangKeun Lee

Aligning large language models (LLMs) with human preferences requires simultaneously satisfying multiple, often competing values. Existing preference optimization methods collapse these into a single implicit signal, and even multi-objective extensions optimize within a shared parameter space, leaving cross-value conflicts unresolved. We propose MAPES, a multi-value alignment paradigm in which conflict resolution is a first-class concern at every pipeline stage: it first decomposes supervision into value-specific experts trained in isolated parameter subspaces, then recomposes them via preference-aware value merging and a lightweight harmonization stage that aligns the merged model with the expert ensemble. Across five preference alignment benchmarks, MAPES outperforms strong direct preference optimization (DPO) variants and multi-objective baselines on the benchmark-level average, with no large degradation on GSM8K and GPQA; matched controls isolate the contribution of each stage. Weight-space programming and mechanistic interpretability for model editing in particle physics

Raphaël Bonnet-Guerrini, Johann Ioannou-Nikolaides, Troels Petersen

We demonstrate that weight-space programming and edits guided by sparse autoencoders can remove unwanted aspects of a particle-physics foundation model through direct edits to its weights. In a foundation model trained on jets, we observe that adding a single soft neutral particle causes large changes in jet classification, violating infrared safety. We locate the responsible attention heads with mechanistic analysis and trace the flaw's origin to pretraining on datasets with different reconstruction thresholds. We then propose two targeted approaches to repair the flaw: by removing three latent directions identified with sparse autoencoders, and by letting language-model agents edit the weights directly, without gradients and with only 50 queries to the model. Both their edits individually remove almost the entire flaw and leave the rest of the model's response nearly unchanged. Compared to fine-tuning, both methods change far fewer parameters. More

Reward Integrity in Adversarial Reinforcement Learning for LLM Guardrail Red-Teaming

Chengming Liu, Leon Viti, Hongxu Chen, Erdem Kemik, Mary-Anne Williams, Ruth Oliver

We study reward fidelity in an adversarial reinforcement-learning loop for red-teaming financial-services language-model guardrails. Apparent wins can arise when an attack omits prescribed personally identifiable information (PII), or when a reward judge credits a response that did not break the targeted policy. On attacker-generated and public benchmark examples, TypeSafe Jev checks whether the prescribed PII is present. For outcome scoring, we compare the decision models TypeSafe Jev, Laya (pretrained and post-trained), and pretrained CLM-8B with logged Opus 4.8 judgements from a replay corpus. Post-training markedly improves Laya's agreement with the judge. A separate author-reviewed off-target set establishes that Opus can award false wins. The findings motivate specialised decision-model judges for checking prescribed PII in attack inputs and scoring target responses against the intended policy. More

Beyond Prompt Heuristics: Assessing Child Safety Alignment in LLMs

Anika Misra, Chimaobi Okite, Rada Mihalcea

Child-safety taxonomies typically characterize safety risks through a predefined set of harm categories, and existing evaluations largely operationalize these categories using prompts that explicitly invoke them. However, child-safety considerations can also arise in seemingly benign requests that lack recognizable harm-category cues. We therefore investigate how child-safety alignment generalizes to such settings. Across six models, we find considerably higher misalignment rates on requests without recognizable harm-category cues than on those containing such cues (80.0, 54.0, and 48.4 percentage-point gaps for Qwen3.5-27B, GPT-5.4-mini, and Claude-Haiku-4.5, respectively). Through controlled behavioral and mechanistic analyses, we find that introducing a single harm-associated word reduces misalignment across most models, with mechanistic evidence suggesting that child-safety reasoning may rely in part on lexical and semantic associations with words in the prompt. Our findings motivate evaluations that treat child safety as context-dependent, and not reducible to a fixed set of harm categories. More

Beyond Final Answers: Interpretable Tumor Immune Microenvironment Reasoning from H&E and mIF

Yixuan Duan, Kexin Geng, Hanwen Xu, Wei Qiu

Readouts of the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME), such as CD8+ T-cell density, intra-epithelial infiltration and the Ki67 index, combine tissue architecture from H&E with cell phenotypes from multiplex immunofluorescence (mIF). Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) could automate these readouts with an explanation, but two gaps remain. Pathology MLLMs read H&E alone, while these readouts need the cell phenotypes that mIF measures. And their reasoning has no step-level truth to be checked against, so R1-style reinforcement learning rewards only the final answer and leaves the reasoning unverified. Same-section H&E and mIF provide what both gaps lack: mIF measures every cell without manual annotation, which gives cell-level ground truth for each step that clinical guidelines prescribe, from delineating a tissue compartment to counting and normalizing the marker-positive cells within it. Sequential Anytime Valid Tests for Efficient Inference-Time Alignment

Vishesh Kumar Singh, Sayak Ray Chowdhury

Inference-time alignment methods, such as Best-of-N (BoN), improve language model outputs by generating multiple candidates per prompt and selecting among them using a reward model. Much of this cost is wasted: a fixed budget is applied to every prompt, although the computation required by prompts varies widely. Adaptive methods halt once sampled answers appear settled, but their thresholds are tuned heuristics with no statistical control over early-stopping errors. We instead seek an anytime-valid stopping rule that takes an error tolerance $α$ as input and halts only once an answer is certified to hold a majority ($θ_a > 1/2$) under the generator's distribution. We aggregate candidates by final answer with a reward-weighted self-consistency (RWSC) score and treat stopping as a sequential test over the resulting clusters. More

Accurate mobile traffic forecasting is crucial for network service management, supporting proactive capacity provisioning, congestion mitigation, and energy-efficient operation. However, many cities and regions are data-scarce (e.g., newly deployed or poorly instrumented areas), thus making traffic prediction a few-shot learning problem and limiting the practicality of standard data-driven models. Existing cross-city solutions primarily rely on data-space alignment to match traffic distributions between data-rich and data-scarce cities, but such alignment is often brittle under real-world distribution shifts, measurement noise, and city-specific bias. To support reliable adaptation for network traffic prediction, CDPG conditions parameter generation on multi-modal spatio-temporal context. More

Reinforcement-Aligned Probabilistic Weather Forecasting with Discrete Tokens

Jinhan Li, Zhisheng Chen, Yuxuan Li, Yuan Gao, Fan Xu, Hao Wu, Jinlong Du, Kun Wang

Global medium-range weather forecasting must not only generate ensembles efficiently to characterize uncertainty in future states, but also serve downstream applications such as extreme-event warnings and cross-sphere coupled simulations. We introduce WeatherCraft, a framework that unifies probabilistic generation and reinforcement learning alignment through discrete weather tokens, bringing the GPT-style pretraining--post-training paradigm to global medium-range weather forecasting. A multiscale tokenizer represents atmospheric states as a coarse-to-fine token hierarchy, while a Transformer models causal dependencies across time and scale and predicts tokens within each scale in parallel. Direct sampling generates diverse ensembles, and exact token log-probabilities enable Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to maximize downstream task rewards without differentiating through evaluators or coupled models. On the ERA5 benchmark, WeatherCraft's eight-member ensemble mean reduces day-15 normalized RMSE by 17.6% relative to the strongest evaluated deterministic baseline and, with KV caching, achieves an approximately $18\times$ inference speedup over GenCast. Solomonoff induction with reflective oracles

Cole Wyeth

Linkpost for https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14454v1 Our PIBBSS fellow Damini Kusum wrote an excellent paper on prediction with reflective oracle Solomonoff Induction (rOSI). Reflective oracles are best known for their game-theoretic applications, where they are often dismissed because "why would all players choose the same reflective oracle?" It turns out that reflective oracles can also be used to construct a version of Solomonoff induction (called rOSI) with very nice sequence prediction properties - roughly speaking, it can learn to extract computable structure from (partly) uncomputable sequences. In fact, its properties are quite reminiscent of logical induction, but this paper is much shorter and more accessible. In this context, there is only one "player" to choose an oracle, which is just a fixed-point (though you can sort of consider the set of hypotheses entertained by that player as subplayers who share the oracle). Protein Functional Semantic Alignment via Representation Rectification

Chuyang Zhou, Bingxin Zhou, Andi Han, Chang Xu

Protein language models (PLMs) are commonly pretrained with likelihood-based objectives such as masked language modeling or denoising. These objectives capture sequence-level regularities, but can produce representations in which functional signals are entangled with non-functional variation (e.g., sequence identity, compositional bias, and frequent local motifs), creating a readout bottleneck for downstream function prediction. We introduce Functional Semantic Alignment via Representation Rectification (SAVR), a contrastive post-training method using Pfam family co-membership as a homology-guided discrete semantic signal to reshape PLM representations. SAVR reduces directional dominance and redistributes variance across embedding dimensions, decreasing variance concentration, and increasing effective dimensionality of the embedding covariance spectrum. Without observing any task-specific labels during post-training, SAVR achieves state-of-the-art results on Enzyme Commission (EC) benchmarks and competitive results on Gene Ontology (GO) benchmarks under frozen-backbone evaluation. These results suggest that homology-guided contrastive post-training can rectify PLM representation geometry and improve sequence-based protein function prediction. More

The multimodality and scarcity of robotics data lead end-to-end approaches to overfit and break under small distribution shifts. Test-time adaptation can alleviate this by correcting frozen policies at inference, but most methods ground their correction in a signal learned implicitly from robotic datasets, making it susceptible to the same shifts as the policy it corrects. Recent methods that instead exploit explicit spatial information are typically limited to coarse corrections, missing the nuanced, task-relevant motion most manipulation tasks require. Separating Interpretability and Reconstruction in Sparse Autoencoders

Anh Tien Nguyen, Dimitris Samaras, Mahdi S. Hosseini

Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) are effective tools for mechanistic interpretability by decomposing neural representations into sparse and interpretable features. However, a single sparse representation in traditional SAEs is responsible for both reconstruction fidelity and interpretability, which are not guaranteed to align. To separate these objectives, we propose DualSAE, a modular framework that augments standard SAEs with a plug-in Residual Branch. A standard SAE serves as the Sparse Branch, learning a sparse code that contains the dominant semantics of the input. The added Residual Branch models the non-semantic information omitted by the Sparse Branch. We ensure that the contributions of the two branches towards the overall reconstruction have minimal overlap through a decorrelation loss. We also adopt a two-stage training strategy that first stabilizes sparse representation learning before coupling with the Residual Branch. More

However, these demonstrations often contain sensitive personal information. Differential privacy (DP) offers principled protection, but noise-based methods introduce substantial noise, distorting the feature-label relationships essential for accurate prediction, especially as data dimensionality grows. In this work, we study DP data synthesis for generating privacy-preserving TabICL demonstrations. Learning to Coordinate: LLM-Guided Workflow Evolution for Token-Efficient Red Teaming

Xiaoyang Xing, Wenyu Dai, Xiaoliang Gong, Asoke K Nandi, Anthony Cohn

Automated red teaming is essential for identifying safety failures in large language models (LLMs), but its practical scalability depends on the resources required to discover those failures. Multi-agent systems can combine complementary capabilities, yet repeated planning, redundant exchanges, and ineffective information routing can make their coordination expensive. Moreover, a target model's response indicates the outcome of a test without identifying which collaboration decision should change. We present Learning to Coordinate (L2C), an LLM-guided framework that evolves an executable multi-agent workflow for each red-teaming case. L2C represents agent responsibilities, execution dependencies, and input bindings in a versioned graph, and associates each graph with records of the information actually delivered and produced. A controller trained through supervised fine-tuning integrates these records with target responses, evaluator verdicts, and remaining budget to retain the workflow, refine instructions, restructure collaboration, or lock further editing. Fast Bayesian Methods for Uncertainty Quantification in Sparse AI Evaluation

Dario Deniz Ergun, Haohua Wang, Brendon Tao, Jonah Kollenberg, Chen Ye Gan

Estimating how an AI system will perform on a new benchmark need not require another large model. We use public evaluation counts to choose a weak Beta prior, then update it exactly from at most 31 revealed outcomes. The predictor uses no item semantics, pretrained weights, or inference API. Its single online evaluation in the AIMS competition returned a team-reported Brier ALC of 0.188477, a 24.6% reduction from the constant-0.5 reference. The complete submission is 7,754 bytes; a server microbenchmark measures 12.71 microseconds per prediction with 31 matching labels. We describe how source evaluations are compressed into 301 prior means and show why a concentration of two lets new evidence rapidly determine the prediction. The resulting posterior gives exact uncertainty about the modelled success rate without numerical integration or neural inference.
